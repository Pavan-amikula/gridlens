# Primary-source research and applicability register

Research date: **25 October 2026**. This register separates binding rules, national implementation, emerging requirements, research, and study assumptions. GridLens is a reproducible screening study; it does not issue a connection approval or replace validated vendor EMT models.

## Research basis and comparable work

**[6. ENTSO-E, Data centres and the power system: expected trends, challenges and opportunities (2026)](https://eepublicdownloads.blob.core.windows.net/public-cdn-container/clean-documents/Reports/2026/FINAL_ENTSO-E_Data_Centres_260430.pdf).** This TSO report discusses fast software-driven demand changes, fault ride-through, ramping, reactive power, oscillation damping and controlled recovery. It supports the selected scenario families and the distinction between operational continuity and grid interaction. Its recommendations are not a directly binding connection code.

**[7. PNNL, Electromagnetic Transient Modeling of Large Data Centers for Grid-Level Studies, PNNL-38817 (16 January 2026)](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/electromagnetic-transient-modeling-large-data-centers-grid-level-studies).** The first-party report describes a generic data-center model library with double-conversion UPS and power-factor-correction interfaces. It explicitly requires additional technical data and expertise to represent a real facility. Use this as the EMT follow-on reference. A balanced positive-sequence screening model cannot establish converter switching, protection coordination, unbalanced faults, PLL stability or harmonic resonance compliance.

**[8. Jimenez-Ruiz and Milano, Data Center Model for Transient Stability Analysis of Power Systems](https://faraday1.ucd.ie/archive/papers/datacenter.pdf).** The authors' public manuscript motivates separate UPS, cooling motor and pulsing IT-load behavior. It supports decomposition of the load; this project does not claim reproduction of the paper's Irish-system model or results. Published version: [10.1049/gtd2.70381](https://doi.org/10.1049/gtd2.70381).

**[9. PNNL, Using Grid-Forming Energy Storage Systems to Provide Dynamic Active Power Support for Hyperscale Data Centers (2026)](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/using-grid-forming-energy-storage-systems-provide-dynamic-active-power-support).** The described EMT study evaluates tuned GFM ESS during low-voltage faults and fast AI-load fluctuations. It is motivation for a controlled-BESS comparator, not evidence that a simplified P/Q-support model implements or validates grid-forming converter control. DOI: [10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3666746](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3666746).

**[10. NERC, Large Loads Action Plan](https://www.nerc.com/initiatives/large-loads-action-plan).** This North American first-party collection includes emerging-large-load characteristics, modeling gaps, incident reviews and mitigation guidance. It supports risk identification, especially correlated voltage-sensitive load reduction. NERC material is an engineering comparator, not a European legal requirement.

## Public datasets, provenance and licenses

**[11. Google, cluster-data / PowerData2019](https://github.com/google/cluster-data).** The repository provides May 2019 cluster traces and power traces for 57 power domains under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The [power documentation](https://github.com/google/cluster-data/blob/master/PowerData2019.md) points to the paper [Data Center Power Oversubscription with a Medium Voltage Power Plane and Priority-Aware Capping](https://research.google/pubs/data-center-power-oversubscription-with-a-medium-voltage-power-plane-and-priority-aware-capping/). The supplied notebook accesses BigQuery and requires a cloud project, authentication and billing. Five-minute power data cannot demonstrate subsecond AI excursions; it is suitable for slower loading studies. It was reviewed, not silently embedded as telemetry.

**[12. MIT Supercloud Dataset](https://github.com/MIT-AI-Accelerator/MIT-Supercloud-Dataset).** The first-party README describes anonymized scheduler, CPU, GPU and node-monitoring data; GPU measurements occur at 100 ms intervals. Paper: [The MIT Supercloud Dataset, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02037), DOI [10.1109/HPEC49654.2021.9622850](https://doi.org/10.1109/HPEC49654.2021.9622850). The [AWS open-data registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/dcc/) specifies **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**. A small public original file was verified on the research date:

- [Original GPU CSV — 157,396 bytes](https://mit-supercloud-dataset.s3.amazonaws.com/datacenter-challenge/202201/gpu/0000/32585007376605-r8939293-n208530.csv).
- [Original license](https://mit-supercloud-dataset.s3.amazonaws.com/datacenter-challenge/202201/LICENSE).
- Fields include timestamp, GPU index, GPU utilization, memory, temperature and power_draw_W.

Preserve the original file, its license and attribution when including an unmodified research sample; do not place third-party data under the software's MIT license. Do not redistribute transformed trace files under an unrestricted license. Scaling one measured GPU trace into a hypothetical 100–500 MW facility is a **synthetic aggregation assumption**, not measured hyperscale POI telemetry. Resampling cannot create new measurement bandwidth. Store provenance, time scaling and power scaling separately from simulator parameters.

## Study design and interface decisions

The following are design decisions inferred from the literature, not claims made by a source. Compare identical disturbances, network strength and loads across baseline, BESS, STATCOM, synchronous-condenser and coordinated cases. Enforce current, MVA, energy and state-of-charge constraints so controls cannot provide unlimited support. Report all scenarios and regressions, rather than only the strongest improvement. Use an explicit solver-convergence check, energy accounting and parameter sensitivity.

The reviewed Fingrid and ENTSO-E sites organize information around requirements, evidence and system behavior. The proposed interface follows that engineering structure: a restrained light workspace, single-line overview, disturbance controls, unit-labeled time series, paired baseline/mitigation metrics and an applicability register. These are original interface choices; no third-party interface assets are reused. Replay is an accelerated visualization of deterministic offline simulation, not a live grid feed or hardware-in-the-loop validation.

### Permissive power-data alternative selected for optional import

[PM100: A Job Power Consumption Dataset of a Large-Scale HPC System](https://zenodo.org/records/10127767), Antici, Seyedkazemi Ardebili, Bartolini and Kiziltan (2023), is a first-party dataset of 231,116 Marconi100 jobs from May–October 2020. It contains node, CPU and memory power records. The Zenodo API identifies its license as **CC BY 4.0**, allowing an attributed derived research subset. The [official generation repository](https://github.com/francescoantici/PM100-data) documents the features and extraction procedure. The anonymous [Parquet download](https://zenodo.org/api/records/10127767/files/job_table.parquet/content) is 287,154,521 bytes; the archive is not a small CSV. Prefer a small reproducibly extracted subset over committing the full file. PM100 is HPC job telemetry, not hyperscale AI POI disturbance measurements. It can inform representative loading but cannot validate a grid dynamic model or provide evidence about subsecond behavior without verifying the underlying sample cadence.
